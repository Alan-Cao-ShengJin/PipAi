# Driver forecast and assumption audit

Build an explicit waterfall from industry/company drivers to segment revenue, gross profit, operating expenses, tax, attributable earnings, cash flow and financing needs. Use a different decomposition when the business requires it. A total-only earnings forecast without an auditable bridge is not a completed driver model.

## Forecast construction

1. Reconcile the historical segment definitions and explain restated or broker-derived splits. Volume x price is meaningful only when their bases match. Reconcile unexplained residuals; do not quietly assign them to other revenue.
2. Anchor assumptions in historical economics, recent operating data, management guidance and independent industry evidence. Label estimates and preserve ranges when precision is unsupported.
3. Model demand, capacity, price/mix, margins and cost timing together. Respect start dates of policy changes, new capacity and products. Historical seasonality is a candidate assumption, not a forecast identity.
4. Derive the remaining quarter from FY minus YTD only when the FY premise is justified. Examine each segment's implied quarter against capacity, demand, seasonality and the latest evidence.
5. Classify recurring versus nonrecurring earnings/cash effects. Zeroing future fair-value gains or disposals can be an explicit scenario assumption, not a universal fact. Model potential impairments and uncertain items as risks/scenarios rather than erasing them.
6. Forecast capex, working capital, debt and liquidity alongside earnings. High growth with no reinvestment or financing need must be justified.

## Incentives, shares and accounting

Read grant/vesting terms, settlement type, performance/service/market conditions, forfeiture assumptions and the applicable accounting framework. Distinguish disclosed expense schedules from analyst estimates; avoid double counting costs already in opex. An incentive hurdle is not a floor under earnings. Never trim costs or reverse an expense simply to make the KPI pass. Analyse multiple outcomes and recognise that different vesting conditions have different accounting effects.

Map share issuance, buybacks, treasury shares and awards into basic/diluted EPS using the issuer's actual treatment. Preserve the timing of changes rather than dividing every period's profit by the latest outstanding shares.

## Audit the assumption register

For every material editable assumption record its definition, value/range, source/available date, rationale, affected output, sensitivity and what would falsify it. Review:

- latest actuals versus stale annualisation;
- structural versus seasonal cost changes;
- implied remaining-quarter demand, margin and costs;
- guidance freshness and metric consistency;
- nonrecurring items and tax reconciliation;
- incentive expense, share count and cash/financing implications;
- FY and segment sums and links into the statements;
- consensus contributor count and post-results freshness;
- differences versus each broker's own accounting/forecast definitions.

Decompose a consensus gap into starting base, volume, price/mix, margins, costs, tax, shares and omitted items. Do not calibrate the model to consensus or decide a broker is wrong solely because it differs. If no comparable consensus exists, say so and continue using clearly attributed scenarios.

Validate downside cases and operational constraints. Mark sensitivity arithmetic as calculation rather than causal proof. Update all dependent valuation/thesis/question/brief artifacts and fixed prose after model revisions.
